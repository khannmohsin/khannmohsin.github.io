Mohsin Khan

UiT The Arctic University of Norway

I am a cybersecurity researcher exploring how to build secure, intelligent, and self-governing distributed systems in a world where billions of devices operate at the edge of the network. My work blends cryptography, embedded security, and blockchain-driven trust to create systems that remain secure even under extreme constraints.

✦ Research Interests

I work at the boundary where distributed systems meet cyber-physical reality, focusing on:

  • Trust enforcement for IoT/OT systems across Edge → Fog → Cloud
  • Smart contract–driven access control and capability-token models
  • Lightweight cryptography and side-channel–aware hardware implementations
  • Threat modeling, vulnerability analysis, and resilient distributed protocols

✦ BlockCap — Rethinking Trust for Distributed IoT

At the heart of my research is BlockCap (Blockchain-Enabled Capability Access Protocol) — a security framework that reimagines how distributed IoT/OT systems establish trust.

BlockCap brings together:

  • Proof-of-Authority consensus
  • Event-driven validator logic
  • Dynamic capability-token policies
  • Secure node bootstrapping
  • Real-time observability
Inspired by NIST 800-183 and OpenFog, BlockCap is designed for systems that are too critical to trust blindly and too constrained to secure traditionally.

✦ Security From the Hardware Up

I benchmark lightweight cryptographic primitives on AVR and ARM microcontrollers using ChipWhisperer, measuring cycles, memory use, energy consumption, and leakage behavior. To evaluate trade-offs across diverse algorithms, I developed E-RANK, a holistic metric that captures efficiency, energy, and structural robustness in a single comparative score.
I also authored a structured survey of 116 lightweight hardware hash functions, mapping their architectures, cryptanalytic insights, and implementation considerations — creating one of the most complete references in the field.

✦ Growing Through Community

My work has been presented at SciSec 2024, NordSec 2025, and SECRYPT 2025, and shaped by advanced research programs across Europe, including:
  • Finse Cyber Security Winter School — University of Oslo
  • Reykjavík Secure Distributed Systems Summer School — Ulm University
  • EuroSys Principles of Consistency Workshop — Rennes, France
Each of these experiences expanded my perspective and connected me with researchers shaping the future of distributed security.

✦ What I’m Exploring Next

My current research extends into post-quantum–resilient IoT systems, hybrid cryptographic models, formal benchmarking standards, and ML-assisted anomaly detection. I am also working to transfer research prototypes into industry-ready, deployable security solutions.
